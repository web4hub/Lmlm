Yes. This is buildable, but I would tighten the blueprint before writing production code. The core idea is strong; several clinical, ML, and regulatory assumptions need correcting so OmniSkel-AI doesn’t accidentally become a research demo masquerading as a clinical product.

Knee-first → measurement-first → radiologist-in-the-loop → multimodal fusion → longitudinal intelligence → full-body foundation.

2. One important architectural change

I would not make this:

DICOM → NIfTI → everything else

the canonical pipeline.

Instead:

DICOM
  │
  ├── preserve original DICOM
  │
  ├── metadata extraction
  │
  ├── de-identification
  │
  ├── quality control
  │
  └── canonical research representation
          │
          ├── NIfTI
          ├── tensors
          └── derived measurements

Keep the original DICOM immutable.

That gives you:

* traceability
* reproducibility
* reprocessing
* regulatory auditability
* scanner/protocol metadata
* future model development

NIfTI should be a derived representation, not the source of truth.

15. Regulatory strategy needs another correction

I would not hard-code:

“OmniSkel-AI is a Class II 510(k) device.”

The regulatory pathway depends on the exact intended use, claims, technological characteristics, predicate availability, and FDA’s determination.

The FDA currently has specific digital-health guidance covering clinical decision-support software and AI-enabled device software, including lifecycle considerations and predetermined change-control plans. 

So the architecture should instead contain:

Regulatory Strategy
        │
        ├── intended use
        ├── indications
        ├── risk classification
        ├── predicate analysis
        ├── clinical evidence
        ├── software lifecycle
        ├── cybersecurity
        └── submission pathway

And then conduct an FDA Pre-Submission/Q-Submission to get feedback before locking the regulatory route. FDA explicitly describes Pre-Submission as a mechanism for obtaining feedback that can guide device development and submission preparation. 

Likewise, don’t assume Breakthrough Designation for the full-body platform. FDA’s Breakthrough program has specific eligibility criteria and is available for devices that meet those criteria; designation does not itself replace the applicable marketing authorization requirements. 

For a knee OA/meniscus product, STeP may also be worth evaluating depending on the eventual device and indication.
